\begin{abstract}
A structured covariance pilot can stabilize prediction while missing dependence that emerges after deployment.  We study strict one-step-ahead covariance forecasting relative to a positive-definite pilot frozen within each deployment episode.  A uniform high-probability radius is appropriate for structural certification but can be too conservative for prediction.  ATLAS-SB therefore has two outputs.  Its predictive output aggregates dyadic rolling-window experts in precision space with prequential exponential weights; its certified output uses a stabilized observable matrix empirical-Bernstein radius and a Lepski selector.  We prove pathwise Gaussian log-loss regret against the best fixed memory, a simultaneous rolling covariance certificate, a locally adaptive H\"older operator-risk rate for the certified selector, and a matching Gaussian rank-one lower bound.  Strict multidimensional simulations show that the certified selector controls false discoveries but can lag after changes, whereas predictive aggregation remains competitive with the best fixed memory.  A public ten-batch gas-sensor study freezes preprocessing and the pilot on batches 1--4 and evaluates batches 5--10 once in chronological order.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Many learning systems deploy a covariance model estimated before operation: a factor model, shrinkage target, sensor baseline, or covariance induced by a pretrained representation.  Such a \emph{pilot} lowers variance and stabilizes downstream decisions.  It can nevertheless omit dependence that appears after deployment, rotates, and later vanishes.  Re-estimating the full model sacrifices stable structure; freezing it without correction miscalibrates predictive likelihoods and quadratic decisions.

We formulate \emph{pilot-preserving covariance forecasting}.  After observing $x_t$, a learner predicts the conditional covariance of $x_{t+1}$ while retaining a frozen positive-definite pilot.  The learner may add positive-semidefinite covariance in directions unexplained by the pilot, but it may not rewrite the pilot or subtract variance that the pilot overstates.  A negative residual therefore rejects the augmentation model instead of becoming another fitted factor.

Adaptive memory creates a statistical conflict.  Uniform confidence radii support honest rank and model-validity statements, but their logarithmic and boundedness terms can delay hard memory switches.  Prediction only requires low cumulative loss.  ATLAS-SB separates these tasks: precision-space prequential aggregation supplies the forecast, while stabilized empirical-Bernstein bounds supply a certified hard selector and structural diagnostics.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(1) We define a strict $t\mapsto t+1$ frozen-pilot residual problem.  (2) We combine an observable matrix empirical-Bernstein radius with a deterministic sub-Gaussian floor.  The stabilized radius is valid and cannot become spuriously small.  (3) We prove Gaussian log-loss regret for precision aggregation, an adaptive Lepski oracle inequality, an expected H\"older operator-risk rate, and a same-risk rank-one lower bound.  (4) We run the exact implemented methods against every fixed candidate, a covariance-space mixture, and an infeasible local oracle in multidimensional simulations and public sensor drift data.  We make no missing-data or unconditional rank-recovery claim.

Related work estimates static robust covariance \citep{ledoit2004well,fan2013poc}, locally varying covariance \citep{chen2013covariance,fryzlewicz2013volatility,dendramis2021timevarying,jung2019timevarying}, and changing subspaces \citep{balzano2010grouse,narayanamurthy2018norst}.  Financial risk-model augmentation uses user-specified transient rank and half-life \citep{tzikas2026transient}.  Our contribution is the combination of a supplied frozen pilot, strict next-period targets, predictive expert aggregation, and separate observable certification.

\section{Frozen-Pilot Experts}
\label{sec:model}

A pre-deployment sample supplies
\begin{equation}
 \Sigma_{\base}=B\Omega B^\top+D\succ0,\qquad D=\diag(d_1,\ldots,d_n)\succ0.
 \label{eq:pilot}
\end{equation}
Let $A=D^{-1/2}B$ and let $Q\in\R^{n\times m}$ span $\operatorname{span}(A)^\perp$.  Within an episode,
\begin{equation}
 y_t=Q^\top D^{-1/2}x_t,\qquad
 C_{t+1}=\E(y_{t+1}y_{t+1}^\top\mid\F_t)=I_m+T_{t+1},
 \label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
where $T_{t+1}\succeq0$.  The original-unit covariance is
$\Sigma_{\base}+D^{1/2}QT_{t+1}Q^\top D^{1/2}$.  Equation~\eqref{eq:target} excludes pilot--residual cross blocks, covariance changes inside the pilot span, and negative corrections.  It is a model restriction, not merely a normalization.

For $\tau>0$, put
\begin{equation}
 Z_s=\frac{\psi_\tau(y_s)\psi_\tau(y_s)^\top}{\tau^2},\qquad
 \psi_\tau(y)=y\min\{1,\tau/\|y\|\}.
\end{equation}
Then $0\preceq Z_s\preceq I$.  On a dyadic grid $\Hh=\{H_1,2H_1,\ldots,H_J\}$, define
\begin{equation}
 S_t(H)=\frac{\tau^2}{H}\sum_{s=t-H+1}^tZ_s,\qquad
 V_t(H)=\sum_{s=t-H+1}^tZ_s^2.
 \label{eq:rolling}
\end{equation}
Each expert covariance is $C_{H,t}=\Pi_{[I,MI]}\{S_t(H)\}$ and its precision is $\Theta_{H,t}=C_{H,t}^{-1}$.  The rolling statistics update in $O(Jm^2)$ time.

\section{Prediction and Certification}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Prequential predictive aggregation}

Let
\begin{equation}
 g_{t+1}(\Theta)=-\log\det\Theta+y_{t+1}^\top\Theta y_{t+1}.
\end{equation}
Starting from uniform weights, update
\begin{equation}
 w_{H,t+1}\propto w_{H,t}\exp\{-\eta g_{t+1}(\Theta_{H,t})\}.
 \label{eq:weights}
\end{equation}
Before $y_{t+1}$ arrives, the predictive output is
\begin{equation}
 \overline\Theta_t=\sum_{H\in\Hh}w_{H,t}\Theta_{H,t},\qquad
 \widehat C_t^{\rm pred}=\overline\Theta_t^{-1}.
 \label{eq:predictive}
\end{equation}
Precision rather than covariance is averaged because Gaussian loss is convex in precision.

\begin{theorem}[Prequential log-loss regret]
\label{thm:regret}
If $\|y_{t+1}\|\le\tau$ and every expert has eigenvalues in $[1,M]$, put
$R=m\log M+\tau^2(1-M^{-1})$.  For every $\eta>0$,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{t=1}^Tg_{t+1}(\overline\Theta_t)-
 \min_H\sum_{t=1}^Tg_{t+1}(\Theta_{H,t})
 \le\frac{\log J}{\eta}+\frac{\eta R^2T}{8}.
 \label{eq:regret}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Convexity gives $g(\overline\Theta_t)\le\sum_Hw_{H,t}g(\Theta_{H,t})$; the rest is the exponential-weights potential argument.  Appendix~\ref{app:aggregation} gives the proof.

\subsection{Stabilized certification}

Let $\phi(\gamma)=-\log(1-\gamma)-\gamma$ and use a fixed finite grid $\Gamma\subset(0,1)$.  With $\ell_\alpha=\log\{8mTJ|\Gamma|/\alpha\}$, define
\begin{equation}
 \widehat e_t(H)=\min_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
 \frac{2\tau^2}{H\gamma}
 \left[\ell_\alpha+\frac{\phi(\gamma)}4\lambda_{\max}\{V_t(H)\}\right].
 \label{eq:emp-radius}
\end{equation}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:sg}, a second valid radius is
\begin{equation}
 r(H)=c_rK^2M\left\{\sqrt{d_\alpha/H}+d_\alpha/H\right\},\quad
 d_\alpha=m+\log(8TJ/\alpha).
 \label{eq:det-radius}
\end{equation}
We use
\begin{equation}
 \rho_t(H)=\max\{\kappa r(H),\min[r(H),\widehat e_t(H)]\}+b_\tau.
 \label{eq:stabilized-radius}
\end{equation}
Let $\overline\rho_t(H)=\max_{G\ge H}\rho_t(G)$.  The certified selector is the largest $H$ satisfying, for all $h\le H$,
\begin{equation}
 \|S_t(H)-S_t(h)\|_{\op}
 \le\overline\rho_t(H)+\overline\rho_t(h)+2\kappa r(h).
 \label{eq:selector}
\end{equation}
Its covariance output is $\widehat C_t^{\rm cert}=\Pi_{[I,MI]}S_t(\widehat H_t)$.  It is reported as a diagnostic rather than substituted for \eqref{eq:predictive}.

\begin{assumption}[Conditional sub-Gaussian residuals]
\label{ass:sg}
There are declared $K\ge1$ and $M>1$ such that $y_s=C_s^{1/2}\xi_s$, $I\preceq C_s\preceq MI$, $\E_{s-1}\xi_s=0$, $\E_{s-1}\xi_s\xi_s^\top=I$, and
$\E_{s-1}\exp(\lambda v^\top\xi_s)\le\exp(K^2\lambda^2/2)$ for every unit $v$ and $\lambda\in\R$.
\end{assumption}

The envelopes $(K,M)$ are declared before evaluation; ATLAS-SB is not parameter free.  Public-data envelopes are calibration quantities and do not imply formal coverage.

\section{Certificate Theory}
\label{sec:theory}

Put $\overline C_t(H)=H^{-1}\sum_{s=t-H+1}^tC_s$ and
$B_t(H)=\|\overline C_t(H)-C_{t+1}\|_{\op}$.

\begin{theorem}[Simultaneous rolling certificate]
\label{thm:certificate}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:sg}, for a sub-Gaussian clipping threshold and sufficient $c_r$, with probability at least $1-\alpha$, simultaneously over $t\le T$ and $H\in\Hh$,
\begin{equation}
 \|S_t(H)-\overline C_t(H)\|_{\op}\le\rho_t(H),\qquad
 \kappa r(H)+b_\tau\le\rho_t(H)\le r(H)+b_\tau.
 \label{eq:certificate}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The proof restarts the matrix empirical-Bernstein supermartingale of \citet{wang2024matrixeb} at each deterministic rolling origin, combines both tails, and intersects it with a standard sub-Gaussian covariance event.

Let $B_t^\uparrow(H)=\max_{h\le H}B_t(h)$ and
$H_t^\circ=\max\{H:B_t^\uparrow(H)\le\kappa r(H)\}$.

\begin{theorem}[Certified-selector oracle inequality]
\label{thm:oracle}
On the event in Theorem~\ref{thm:certificate},
\begin{equation}
 \|\widehat C_t^{\rm cert}-C_{t+1}\|_{\op}
 \le12\overline\rho_t(H_t^\circ).
 \label{eq:oracle}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

If locally
$\|C_{t-j}-C_{t+1}\|_{\op}\le L_t(j+1)^\beta$, then $B_t(H)\le c_\beta L_tH^\beta$.

\begin{corollary}[Adaptive expected operator risk]
\label{cor:rate}
If the dyadic grid contains
$H_t^\star\asymp(K^4M^2d_\alpha/L_t^2)^{1/(2\beta+1)}\gtrsim d_\alpha$, then
\begin{equation}
 \E\|\widehat C_t^{\rm cert}-C_{t+1}\|_{\op}
 \le C_\beta(K^4M^2d_\alpha)^{\beta/(2\beta+1)}L_t^{1/(2\beta+1)}
 +C_\beta b_\tau+\alpha(M-1).
 \label{eq:upper-rate}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Same-risk lower bound]
\label{thm:lower}
For independent Gaussian streams $C_s=I+\theta_svv^\top$ with unknown unit $v$, $0\le\theta_s\le1/4$, and $|\theta_s-\theta_u|\le L|s-u|^\beta$,
\begin{equation}
 \inf_{\widetilde C_t}\sup_P\E_P\|\widetilde C_t-C_{t+1}\|_{\op}
 \ge cL^{1/(2\beta+1)}m^{\beta/(2\beta+1)}
 \label{eq:lower-rate}
\end{equation}
through the nontrivial interior regime.
\end{theorem}

Thus the certified selector is minimax adaptive up to logarithms under its stated assumptions, while Theorem~\ref{thm:regret} protects predictive aggregation against its finite-sample conservatism.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Strict multidimensional Monte Carlo}

We generate bounded streams $y_t=C_t^{1/2}\epsilon_t$ with Rademacher coordinates.  Four paths cover stationarity, smooth rotation, birth/death, and a mixed path with rotation, a rank-two burst, a reset, and fast motion.  We use $m\in\{4,8,16\}$, every window in $\{256,512,1024,2048\}$, and common seeds.  Forecasts use observations through $t$ and are scored only against $C_{t+1}$ and $y_{t+1}$.  Baselines include every fixed window, covariance-space aggregation, the pilot, the certified selector, and an infeasible local oracle.

\section{Diagnostics and Limitations}
\label{sec:diagnostics}

If an external envelope $\overline B_t(H)\ge B_t(H)$ is available, then
$\varepsilon_t=\rho_t(\widehat H_t)+\overline B_t(\widehat H_t)$ gives the conservative lower bound
\begin{equation}
 \#\{i:\lambda_i(S_t(\widehat H_t))>1+2\varepsilon_t\}
 \le\rank(T_{t+1}).
\end{equation}
A smallest eigenvalue below $1-\varepsilon_t$ rejects the one-sided model.  The stochastic radius alone cannot certify rank because it excludes drift.

The pilot must remain fixed within an episode.  Formal certificates require valid $(K,M)$ envelopes and complete residual observations.  PSD augmentation cannot repair pilot overstatement, cross-block misspecification, or changes inside the pilot span.  The H\"older result is an interior-regime statement.  Public-data envelopes are empirical, only six held-out batch origins are available, and target-batch covariance is noisy.  Precision aggregation competes with the best fixed memory, not a time-varying oracle.

\section{Conclusion}

ATLAS-SB separates two roles that one adaptive threshold cannot safely perform.  Prequential precision aggregation supplies low-loss forecasts; stabilized empirical-Bernstein bounds supply structural certificates and a conservative hard selector.  This separation preserves the frozen pilot, keeps every forecast positive definite, and turns certificate conservatism into an audited limitation rather than a hidden tuning choice.

\section*{Impact Statement}
Covariance forecasts affect automated allocation, monitoring, and control.  Positive augmentation can reduce omitted-risk errors but cannot fix an overstated pilot.  We therefore report model-failure diagnostics, certificate coverage, and infeasible-oracle comparisons, and do not interpret the financial motivation as investment advice.
