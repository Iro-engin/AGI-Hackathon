\section{Model approximation, calibration, and interpretation}
\label{sec:calibration}

The exact model in \eqref{eq:full-target} isolates the statistical problem.  This section characterizes what the one-sided correction estimates when the model is only approximate, explains how the radii enter an implementation, and distinguishes predictive output from structural certification.

\subsection{Best correction when the pilot is misspecified}

For an arbitrary target covariance, define the standardized discrepancy
\begin{equation}
 M_{t+1}=D^{-1/2}(\Sigma_{t+1}-\Sigma_{\base})D^{-1/2}.
 \label{eq:standardized-discrepancy}
\end{equation}
The class of admissible pilot-preserving covariances is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal C_{\base}=\left\{\Sigma_{\base}+D^{1/2}QGQ^\top D^{1/2}:G\succeq0\right\}.
 \label{eq:pilot-class}
\end{equation}
This class excludes corrections in the pilot span, cross-block corrections, and negative residual covariance.

\begin{proposition}[Best one-sided approximation]
\label{prop:best-approximation}
The Frobenius projection of $M_{t+1}$ onto $\{QGQ^\top:G\succeq0\}$ is
\begin{equation}
 G_{t+1}^\dagger=\{Q^\top M_{t+1}Q\}_+.
 \label{eq:best-correction}
\end{equation}
Let
$R_{t+1}=M_{t+1}-QG_{t+1}^\dagger Q^\top$.  Then $\|R_{t+1}\|_{\fro}$ is the irreducible standardized approximation error over $\mathcal C_{\base}$, and every pilot-preserving forecast obeys
\begin{equation}
 \left\|D^{-1/2}(\widehat\Sigma_t-\Sigma_{t+1})D^{-1/2}\right\|_{\fro}
 \le \|\widehat G_t-G_{t+1}^\dagger\|_{\fro}+\|R_{t+1}\|_{\fro},
 \label{eq:approximation-decomp}
\end{equation}
where $\widehat G_t=\widehat C_t-I_m$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Complete $Q$ to an orthogonal basis $[Q_\perp,Q]$ and express $M_{t+1}$ in the resulting blocks.  All blocks except the $Q$--$Q$ block are constant in
$\|M_{t+1}-QGQ^\top\|_{\fro}^2$.  The remaining problem is
$\min_{G\succeq0}\|Q^\top M_{t+1}Q-G\|_{\fro}^2$, whose solution is the spectral positive part.  Equation~\eqref{eq:approximation-decomp} follows from the triangle inequality.
\end{proof}

Under the exact model, $R_{t+1}=0$ and $G_{t+1}^\dagger=T_{t+1}$.  Under misspecification, the statistical theorems control estimation of the admissible positive residual, while $R_{t+1}$ records the modeling error.  The negative-residual flag detects a sufficiently large negative component in the $Q$--$Q$ block.  It cannot detect a discrepancy confined to the pilot span or a cross block from residual observations alone; an episode-level pilot audit must address those components.

Moment and tail envelopes can come from domain restrictions or an independent pre-deployment sample.  Estimating them online from the same path is possible, but requires a self-normalized matrix inequality and changes the theorem.  We therefore keep the certified version explicit.  The crude bounded-increment radius is always implementable once $(M_{2+\delta},\delta,\tau)$ are declared; it can be conservative in high dimension.  The sub-Gaussian radius is sharper and drives the minimax result.  A practical system may expose both a prediction-oriented output and a certified output, provided it labels them separately.

\subsection{Why memory must adapt locally}

Ignoring constants and the clipping term, the one-step risk proxy under \eqref{eq:holder} is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R_t(H)\asymp A\sqrt{d_T/H}+L_tH^\beta.
 \label{eq:risk-proxy}
\end{equation}
The first term decreases with memory and the second increases.  On a stable interval with $L_t=0$, the longest admissible memory is optimal.  As $L_t$ grows, the minimizer moves to
$H_t^\star\asymp(A^2d_T/L_t^2)^{1/(2\beta+1)}$ and eventually reaches the shortest grid point.  A single half-life can therefore be near-oracle on both intervals only when the grid is effectively degenerate or the path never changes its local scale.  The Lepski comparison detects when long- and short-memory scatters cease to agree beyond uncertainty, which is the observable implication of the bias term in \eqref{eq:risk-proxy}.

This interpretation also clarifies the difference from change-point detection.  No discrete changepoint is needed: a smoothly rotating or strengthening factor gradually changes the largest acceptable memory.  After an abrupt event, Proposition~\ref{prop:delay} describes the additional delay caused by exponential averaging and the certification threshold.

The rank output is intentionally a lower confidence bound.  Directions below the threshold may still improve quasi-likelihood, but the data do not support calling them factors at the stated confidence level.  Conversely, the invalidity flag is diagnostic rather than corrective: positive-semidefinite augmentation cannot repair an overstated pilot without changing the model class.

\subsection{Dynamic decision regret}

Proposition~\ref{prop:decision} turns local operator error into a cumulative downstream guarantee.

\begin{corollary}[Dynamic quadratic-decision regret]
\label{cor:dynamic-regret}
Under the conditions of Corollary~\ref{cor:holder}, on its simultaneous event,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\left\{
 \widehat w_t^\top\Sigma_{t+1}\widehat w_t
 -w_t^{\star\top}\Sigma_{t+1}w_t^\star\right\}
 \le C L_w^2\|D\|_{\op}
 \sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\left[
 (A^2d_T)^{\beta/(2\beta+1)}L_t^{1/(2\beta+1)}+b_\tau
 \right].
 \label{eq:dynamic-regret}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply \eqref{eq:decision-transfer} at every origin, substitute \eqref{eq:holder-rate}, and sum.
\end{proof}

The bound is dynamic rather than static-regret relative to one fixed decision.  It compares each action with the oracle that knows the next covariance, so the local path variation appears through $L_t$.  This is the relevant benchmark when a covariance forecast feeds a repeatedly solved controller or allocation problem.

\subsection{Proof architecture}

The upper and lower analyses use the same bias--variance scale.  Theorem~\ref{thm:concentration} decomposes the error into a centered weighted matrix martingale, clipping bias, and the distance from historical covariances to $C_{t+1}$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:oracle} shows that the largest mutually consistent memory is no shorter than the oracle crossing of stochastic error and drift.  Corollary~\ref{cor:holder} evaluates that crossing for a H\"older path.

For the lower bound, a packing of rank-one projectors supplies exponentially many covariance directions.  A temporal bump of width $h$ and terminal height $a=Lh^\beta$ produces operator separation of order $a$, while the entire observed history carries Kullback--Leibler information of order $ha^2$.  Setting $ha^2$ proportional to the logarithm of the packing size, which is order $m$, yields exactly the scale in \eqref{eq:holder-rate}.  Thus the memory selected by the upper bound has the same order as the least favorable temporal window in the lower bound.
