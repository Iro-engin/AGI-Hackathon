\section{Prequential Precision Aggregation}
\label{app:aggregation}

For expert $j$ at origin $t$, let $C_{j,t}$ have eigenvalues in $[1,M]$ and put $\Theta_{j,t}=C_{j,t}^{-1}$.  Define
\begin{equation}
 g_t(\Theta)=-\log\det\Theta+y_{t+1}^\top\Theta y_{t+1}.
\end{equation}
The function $g_t$ is convex on the positive-definite cone.  Starting from $w_{j,1}=1/J$, exponential weights use
\begin{equation}
 w_{j,t+1}=\frac{w_{j,t}\exp\{-\eta g_t(\Theta_{j,t})\}}
 {\sum_{k=1}^Jw_{k,t}\exp\{-\eta g_t(\Theta_{k,t})\}},
\end{equation}
and the ATLAS-SB predictive output is
\begin{equation}
 \overline\Theta_t=\sum_{j=1}^Jw_{j,t}\Theta_{j,t},\qquad
 \widehat C_t^{\rm pred}=\overline\Theta_t^{-1}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Prequential log-loss regret]
\label{thm:aggregation-app}
Suppose $\|y_{t+1}\|\le\tau$ and every expert covariance has eigenvalues in $[1,M]$.  Let
\begin{equation}
 R=m\log M+\tau^2(1-M^{-1}).
\end{equation}
For every $\eta>0$,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{t=1}^T g_t(\overline\Theta_t)
 -\min_{1\le j\le J}\sum_{t=1}^T g_t(\Theta_{j,t})
 \le \frac{\log J}{\eta}+\frac{\eta R^2T}{8}.
 \label{eq:aggregation-regret-app}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Convexity gives
$g_t(\overline\Theta_t)\le\sum_jw_{j,t}g_t(\Theta_{j,t})$.
For $M^{-1}I\preceq\Theta\preceq I$, the log-determinant term lies in $[0,m\log M]$ and the quadratic term lies in
$[\|y_{t+1}\|^2/M,\|y_{t+1}\|^2]$.  Hence the range of expert losses at any time is at most $R$.  Apply Hoeffding's lemma to the usual exponential-weights potential:
\begin{equation}
 \log\sum_jw_{j,t}e^{-\eta g_{j,t}}
 \le-\eta\sum_jw_{j,t}g_{j,t}+\eta^2R^2/8.
\end{equation}
Summing, telescoping the log normalizers, and comparing with the term of any fixed expert $j$ yields
\begin{equation}
 \sum_t\sum_jw_{j,t}g_{j,t}-\sum_tg_{j,t}
 \le\frac{\log J}{\eta}+\frac{\eta R^2T}{8}.
\end{equation}
Combine this inequality with convexity and minimize over $j$.  \end{proof}

The theorem compares with the best fixed memory in hindsight, not the time-varying local oracle.  The certified Lepski selector and the predictive aggregate therefore answer different questions: the former supplies high-probability structural statements, while the latter supplies finite-sample predictive regret.
